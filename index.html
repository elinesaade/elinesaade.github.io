Squichi

A wellbeing app that makes everyday support feel simpler, warmer and more approachable. The name comes from “squishy”: soft, comforting and a little impossible not to squeeze.

Type
Mobile app
Role
UX/UI, art direction, character design, illustration
Focus
Mental wellbeing
Challenge

Make support feel human.

Mental wellbeing apps can feel clinical, overwhelming or overly serious. Squichi needed to be reassuring and playful while staying clear, intuitive and easy to navigate.

Idea

Characters as emotional anchors.

The characters aren't decoration. They encourage, reassure, celebrate progress and guide the user through small everyday actions, giving the app presence and personality.

Unique-corn

One character, fifty identities. A character exploration built around a single unicorn, reimagined through fifty different personalities.

Type
Character series
Role
Character design, art direction, illustration
Variations
50
Meet one

Who is it today?

Same unicorn, different attitude. Shuffle to meet another identity.

Challenge

How far can a character change before it stops being itself?

Fifty variations meant balancing consistency and surprise. Each needed an identity of its own without becoming repetitive or losing what made Unique-corn recognisable.

Process

Same core, new attitude. Your turn.

Every version plays with attitude, styling, colour and detail. The aim was never just a new outfit: each variation suggests a mood, an attitude, maybe even a story. Now dress one yourself.

A co-op game following three siblings through an unconscious world shaped by memories, emotions and everything left unsaid.

Type
Narrative co-op game
Role
Game art, art direction, character design, illustration
Structure
6 chapters
Story

Healing a mind, one dream at a time.

After Michael falls into a coma, his siblings Maria and Mark enter a surreal dream world shaped by his unconscious mind.

Each of the six chapters reveals a wound in Michael's mind. Every puzzle they solve stitches that wound, and through co-op play the siblings reconnect and learn more about each other.

Challenge

Drawing what you can't see.

Abstract emotions, memories and relationships had to become something visual and playable. A feeling could become a creature, a memory a landscape, a relationship could change the colour of a scene.

Colour

Blue for dreams, green for reality.

Most of the game happens in dreams at night, so blue leads, for calm, sadness, melancholy and trust. Green marks reality: life and renewal, in quiet contrast.
